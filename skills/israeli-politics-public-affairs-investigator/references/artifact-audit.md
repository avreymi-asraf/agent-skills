# Visual and geospatial artifact audit

Use this procedure for every downloaded document scan, chart, table, infographic, map, or social screenshot.

## 1. Capture and provenance

Record:

- issuing authority or account;
- title, date, version, document or map identifier;
- publication URL and capture date;
- original resolution and file format;
- whether the file is a draft, final, crop, screenshot, translation or unverified leak;
- source chain and the point at which the file left an official system.

If a screenshot is the only copy, say exactly how it differs from the underlying primary record and seek the original.

## 2. Document scans and leaked letters

Extract verbatim:

- letterhead and issuing office;
- date and reference number;
- addressees and signatories;
- operative clauses;
- attachments and annexes;
- redactions and missing pages;
- stamps, seals, signatures or authentication indicators.

Compare typography, numbering, file names, form language and docket identifiers against authenticated examples from the same office. Do not infer authenticity from visual similarity alone.

## 3. Charts and tables

Extract:

- exact series names and values;
- units, currency basis, nominal or real basis;
- sample size and data vintage;
- axis limits and baselines;
- start and end dates;
- seasonality or revisions;
- whether missing data, breaks or forecast zones are marked.

Audit cherry-picking, truncated axes, non-zero baselines, conflicting scales, absolute-versus-relative claims, and changes in classification or denominator. Reproduce the visual against the underlying table if possible.

## 4. Maps

Record:

- issuing authority and date;
- projection and scale;
- legend keys and line styles;
- boundary status: legal, administrative, proposed, claimed or illustrative;
- whether COGAT, military, municipal, NGO, cadastral or electoral layers are included;
- Areas A/B/C, municipal jurisdictions, security corridors, evacuation zones, infrastructure routes or polling districts when relevant.

Compare at least two independently issued versions for consequential discrepancies. A map may accurately draw a layer while omitting a legally or operationally controlling layer.

## 5. OCR and visual extraction

Keep OCR output local and preserve uncertainty. Mark illegible or ambiguous words rather than silently reconstructing them. If possible, store page images, PDF text, and extracted Markdown or CSV under the dossier.

For PDF claims, read surrounding paragraphs and schedules. For tables, preserve row and column headers. For maps, preserve a close-up legend or scale view.

## 6. Interpretation gate

An artifact earns a stronger conclusion only after:

- provenance is identified;
- the relevant text or data is transcribed;
- the full source or competing official version is compared;
- omissions and distortions are stated;
- the exact claim supported is written separately from unsupported inference.
